How the Lackenby section shift changes CP and LCB

The sectional area curve of the KCS parent, reshaped step by step to CB 0.690 and LCB −1.00 % L, the default case of Demo 8. Every number on the board is computed from the KCS offsets at T = 10.8 m.

What is made

A variant of the KCS hull. Its sections are moved along the length, forebody and aftbody separately, and the gap opened at midship is filled with new parallel middle body. No section is redrawn.

Objective

Reach a target fullness and centre of buoyancy at the parent's L, B and T: CB 0.653 → 0.690 (CP 0.663 → 0.700) and LCB −1.50 → −1.00 % L, by finding the two shift factors cF and cA.

Why these two: the displacement equation Δ = ρ·L·B·T·CB fixes the CB the hull must have; the LCB is set for resistance and must lie under the LCG for an even keel. A parent rarely has both, and CP and LCB govern the wave resistance at given L, B and T. The values here are Demo 8's example inputs; in a project they come from your own design.

Final consequence

A fuller hull with 24.8 m of new parallel middle body (15.4 m forward, 9.4 m aft). Same draught, 10.8 m; displacement +5.7 %, 52,226 → 55,190 m³. Then stretched to the design, here 200 × 30.0 × 10.5 m: 43,470 m³, with CB 0.690 and LCB −1.00 % L kept.
