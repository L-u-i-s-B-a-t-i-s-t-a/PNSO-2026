Quiz 2 · 1 · Length and the Froude number · L2.3acalculator

The 596 TEU feeder has L = 113 m. What is its Froude number at 16 kn (1 kn = 0.5144 m/s)?

  1. AFn ≈ 0.48
  2. BFn ≈ 0.25✓ answer
  3. CFn ≈ 0.07
  4. DFn ≈ 0.77
Solution
  1. V = 16 · 0.5144 = 8.23 m/s.
  2. √(g L) = √(9.81 · 113) = 33.3 m/s.
  3. Fn = V / √(g L) = 8.23 / 33.3 = 0.25.
Why not the others
  • A. 0.48 uses the speed in knots.
  • C. 0.07 divides by L instead of √(g L).
  • D. 0.77 forgets g.
Remember: Fn = V / √(g L), with V in m/s.
Quiz 2 · 2 · The full wave along the hull · Holtrop–Mennencalculator

A ship 100 m long sails so that one full wave of her own system fits between the fore and the aft peak (wavelength λ = L). A deep-water wave has speed V = √(g λ / 2π). What speed is this (1 kn = 0.5144 m/s)?

  1. A≈ 12.5 kn
  2. B≈ 24.3 kn✓ answer
  3. C≈ 17.2 kn
  4. D≈ 34.3 kn
Solution
  1. λ = L = 100 m.
  2. V = √(g λ / 2π) = √(9.81 · 100 / 6.283) = √156.1 = 12.5 m/s.
  3. 12.5 / 0.5144 = 24.3 kn (Fn = 1/√(2π) = 0.40, the classical hull speed).
Why not the others
  • A. 12.5 is the speed in m/s, read as knots.
  • C. 17.2 kn is half a wave (λ = L/2).
  • D. 34.3 kn is λ = 2L.
Remember: One wave per ship length: Fn ≈ 0.40, far above economical speed.
Quiz 2 · 3 · Length against resistance · L2.3acalculator

A slow ship (Fn ≈ 0.19) has friction at 70 % of the total resistance; the other 30 % is residuary (wave and form) resistance. It is made 10 % longer at the same displacement and speed: the friction resistance grows ×1.03 and the residuary resistance falls ×0.87. What is the new total resistance, relative to the old?

  1. A× 1.03, friction wins
  2. B× 0.90, resistance follows the length
  3. C× 0.98, a saving of about 2 %✓ answer
  4. D× 0.87, the waves decide everything
Solution
  1. New total = 0.70 · 1.03 + 0.30 · 0.87.
  2. = 0.721 + 0.261 = 0.982: about 2 % less.
Why not the others
  • A. Friction grows, but the waves fall more.
  • B. Resistance does not follow the length directly.
  • D. Only 30 % of the resistance falls by 13 %.
Remember: At low Fn friction dominates: length gains little.
Quiz 2 · 4 · Length costs steel · L2.3acalculator

Lengthening the reference ship by 10 % (same Δ) cuts the resistance, and so the fuel burned, by 3 %, and raises the hull steel by 15 %. The ship burns fuel worth 1.5 M€ per year and the hull steel costs 8 M€. Over 20 years of operation (no discounting), what is the net change in cost of the longer ship?

  1. A+0.3 M€: the longer ship costs more✓ answer
  2. B−0.9 M€: the longer ship costs less
  3. C−0.3 M€: the longer ship costs less
  4. D+1.2 M€: the longer ship costs more
Solution
  1. Fuel saved over 20 years: 3 % · 1.5 M€ · 20 = 0.9 M€.
  2. Extra steel: 15 % · 8 M€ = 1.2 M€.
  3. Net: 1.2 − 0.9 = +0.3 M€: the longer ship costs more.
Why not the others
  • B. −0.9 M€ counts only the fuel.
  • C. The sign is wrong.
  • D. +1.2 M€ counts only the steel.
Remember: Compare building plus running cost, not one of them.
Quiz 2 · 5 · Beam and draught · L2.3acalculator

Reference ship: Δ = 13,200.7 t, L = 124.8 m, C_B = 0.749, ρ = 1.025 t/m³, B/T = 2.90. What draught do these give?

  1. AT ≈ 6.89 m✓ answer
  2. BT ≈ 11.74 m
  3. CT ≈ 6.98 m
  4. DT ≈ 47.5 m
Solution
  1. ∇ = Δ / ρ = 13,200.7 / 1.025 = 12,879 m³.
  2. B·T = ∇ / (L C_B) = 12,879 / (124.8 · 0.749) = 137.8 m².
  3. B = 2.90 T → 2.90 T² = 137.8 → T² = 47.5 → T = 6.89 m; B = 19.98 m.
Why not the others
  • B. 11.74 m is √(B·T).
  • C. 6.98 m uses Δ in place of ∇.
  • D. 47.5 is T², not T.
Remember: Use the volume ∇, not the mass Δ.
Quiz 2 · 6 · Form coefficients · L2.3a

What is the prismatic coefficient C_P of a hull?

  1. AThe waterplane area divided by L · B
  2. BThe midship section area divided by B · T
  3. CThe displaced volume ∇ divided by the midship section area A_M times the length L: C_P = ∇ / (A_M · L) = C_B / C_M✓ answer
  4. DThe displaced volume ∇ divided by L · B · T
Solution
  1. C_P = ∇ / (A_M · L) = C_B / C_M.
  2. It tells how the volume is spread along the length: low C_P = fine ends, suits faster ships.
  3. Example: C_B = 0.65, C_M = 0.965 → C_P = 0.674.
Why not the others
  • A. That is C_WP.
  • B. That is C_M.
  • D. That is C_B.
Remember: C_P = C_B / C_M.
Quiz 2 · 7 · The six parts · Holtrop–Mennen

Which list gives the parts of the total resistance R_T in the Holtrop–Mennen method?

  1. AFriction, waves, air drag, sea margin, fouling and propeller thrust
  2. BFriction (with the form factor), appendages, waves, bulb, transom and the model–ship correlation allowance✓ answer
  3. CFriction, waves and the propeller thrust deduction only
  4. DFriction, appendages, waves and the wind drag of the superstructure
Solution
  1. R_T = R_F (1 + k1) + R_APP + R_W + R_B + R_TR + R_A.
  2. Friction with form factor, appendages, waves, bulb, transom, correlation allowance.
Why not the others
  • A. Margins, air drag and thrust are not in R_T.
  • C. Thrust deduction belongs to the propulsion chain.
  • D. Wind drag is added afterwards, not in the six parts.
Remember: Six parts: F(1+k1), APP, W, B, TR, A.
Quiz 2 · 8 · Friction · Holtrop–Mennen

How is the friction resistance computed in the method?

  1. AFrom the wave-making coefficient of the hull, with no Reynolds number
  2. BFrom C_F = 0.075 / (log10 Re − 2)² on the wetted surface, with no form factor, because the hull is a flat plate
  3. CFrom the ITTC-1957 line, C_F = 0.075 / (log10 Re − 2)², on the wetted surface, multiplied by the form factor (1 + k1)✓ answer
  4. DFrom the entrance angle iE and the bulb area A_BT
Solution
  1. C_F = 0.075 / (log₁₀ Re − 2)² (ITTC-1957), with Re = V L / ν.
  2. R_F = ½ ρ V² S C_F on the wetted surface S.
  3. The hull is not a flat plate: multiply by the form factor (1 + k1).
Why not the others
  • A. Friction depends on Re.
  • B. The form factor is missing.
  • D. Entrance angle and bulb act on the waves.
Remember: Friction = flat plate × (1 + k1).
Quiz 2 · 9 · Speed and power where friction dominates · Holtrop–Mennencalculator

A 100 m ship sails in the low Froude regime (Fn < 0.2), where the resistance is almost all friction. The friction coefficient C_F = 0.075 / (log10 Re − 2)² is 0.001703 at 10 kn and 0.001663 at 12 kn. How do the friction resistance R_F and the effective power P_E vary with the speed V? Use the two values of C_F to see how it changes with the speed.

Advanced: finding the exponent from two points is the slope of a log-log plot. This goes beyond what the Evaluation Test asks: you do not need it for the test. The video Logarithms and the slope n (portal, Tools and resources) explains it.

  1. AR_F ∝ V² and P_E ∝ V³ exactly, because C_F is constant
  2. BR_F ∝ V^3 and P_E ∝ V^4
  3. CR_F ∝ V^1.9 and P_E ∝ V^2.9: a little below the square and the cube✓ answer
  4. DR_F ∝ V^5 and P_E ∝ V^6, as near a wave hump
Solution
  1. Speed ratio 12/10 = 1.2; C_F ratio 0.001663 / 0.001703 = 0.977.
  2. Exponent of C_F with V: ln 0.977 / ln 1.2 = −0.13.
  3. R_F = ½ρ S C_F V² ∝ V^(2 − 0.13) = V^1.87; P_E = R_F · V ∝ V^2.87.
Why not the others
  • A. C_F is not constant: it falls slowly with Re.
  • B. Friction does not grow with V³.
  • D. V⁵ or more belongs to waves near a hump.
Remember: Friction: a little below V²; power a little below V³.
Quiz 2 · 10 · The three parts of the wave resistance · Holtrop–Mennen

The wave resistance is R_W = c1 c2 c5 ∇ ρ g · exp{ m1 Fn^−0.9 + m2 cos(λ Fn^−2) }. The coefficient m1 is negative (−2.13 for the paper ship) and λ is a constant of the hull (0.65). As the speed grows, which description of the three parts is right?

  1. AThe factor c1 c2 c5 ∇ρg gives the humps and hollows; the term m1 Fn^−0.9 gives the level; the term m2 cos(λ Fn^−2) gives the smooth rise with speed
  2. BThe factor c1 c2 c5 ∇ρg is the wave resistance of the bulb; m1 Fn^−0.9 is that of the transom; m2 cos(λ Fn^−2) is that of the bow
  3. CThe factor c1 c2 c5 ∇ρg does not depend on the speed and sets the level (volume, entrance, bulb, transom); the term m1 Fn^−0.9 rises smoothly with speed; the term m2 cos(λ Fn^−2) swings between plus and minus and gives the humps and hollows✓ answer
  4. DThe three parts are friction, form and correlation; the Froude number only scales them
Solution
  1. c1 c2 c5 ∇ρg does not depend on speed: it sets the level (volume, entrance, bulb, transom).
  2. m1 Fn^(−0.9) with m1 < 0 rises smoothly towards 0 as Fn grows: the smooth growth.
  3. m2 cos(λ Fn^(−2)) swings between + and −: the humps and hollows (hump near Fn = 0.26, hollow near 0.32).
Why not the others
  • A. The roles are swapped.
  • B. The terms are not bulb, transom and bow.
  • D. These are not friction terms.
Remember: Level · smooth rise · oscillation.
Quiz 2 · 11 · Entrance angle · Holtrop–Mennencalculator

The entrance angle iE (half the angle of the design waterline at the bow) appears in the method only in the coefficient c1 of the wave resistance R_W = c1 c2 c5 ∇ρg · exp{…}, with c1 ∝ (90 − iE)^−1.376 (iE in degrees). All the other factors stay the same. What happens to R_W if iE goes from 8° to 16°?

  1. AR_W rises by about 15 %✓ answer
  2. BR_W falls by about 13 %
  3. CR_W doubles, because the angle doubles
  4. DR_W does not change: the entrance angle acts only on the friction
Solution
  1. R_W ∝ c1 ∝ (90 − iE)^(−1.376).
  2. R_W(16°) / R_W(8°) = ((90 − 8) / (90 − 16))^1.376 = (82/74)^1.376 = 1.108^1.376 = 1.152.
  3. R_W rises by about 15 %: a blunter bow.
Why not the others
  • B. 13 % is the ratio turned upside down.
  • C. The angle enters inside (90 − iE) with a power.
  • D. iE acts on the wave resistance.
Remember: Blunter bow, more wave resistance.
Quiz 2 · 12 · The bulb · Holtrop–Mennencalculator

For the paper ship the bulb gives c2 = 0.759. In the wave resistance R_W = c1 c2 c5 ∇ρg · exp{…}, c2 is the factor that carries the bulb: with c2 = 1 there is no bulb. What does this bulb do to R_W, all the other factors being equal?

  1. AR_W rises by about 24 %
  2. BR_W falls by about 76 %
  3. CR_W does not change: the bulb only adds the pressure term R_B
  4. DR_W falls by about 24 %✓ answer
Solution
  1. R_W with bulb = c2 · R_W without bulb = 0.759 · R_W.
  2. 1 − 0.759 = 0.241 → R_W falls by about 24 %.
  3. Price: more wetted surface and a small pressure term R_B.
Why not the others
  • A. The sign is wrong.
  • B. 76 % would be c2 = 0.24.
  • C. c2 multiplies R_W directly.
Remember: c2 < 1: the bulb cuts the wave resistance.
Quiz 2 · 13 · The transom · Holtrop–Mennen

The transom resistance is R_TR = ½ ρ V² A_T c6, with c6 = 0.2 (1 − 0.2 Fn_T) for Fn_T < 5 and c6 = 0 for Fn_T ≥ 5. Fn_T = V / √(2 g A_T / (B + B C_WP)) uses the transom area A_T, a fixed input of the hull. For the paper ship R_TR is 34 kN at 15 kn (7 % of R_T) and zero from 23 kn. Why?

  1. ABecause the transom area A_T grows with the speed
  2. BBecause the bulb takes over the transom resistance above 23 kn
  3. CBecause Fn_T reaches 5 at 23 kn: c6 becomes 0, the transom is dry and the flow leaves it cleanly✓ answer
  4. DBecause the method is not valid above 23 kn
Solution
  1. Fn_T = V / √(2 g A_T / (B + B C_WP)) grows with speed; A_T is fixed.
  2. When Fn_T ≥ 5, c6 = 0: the transom runs dry and the flow leaves it cleanly.
  3. For the paper ship this happens at 23 kn, so R_TR = 0 from there.
Why not the others
  • A. A_T is the immersed transom area at rest: a fixed input.
  • B. The bulb has its own term; it does not take over.
  • D. The method is valid there.
Remember: Dry transom above Fn_T = 5: no transom resistance.
Quiz 2 · 14 · Total resistance and effective power · L2.4

What is the total resistance R_T of a ship, and what do you get by multiplying R_T by the ship speed V?

  1. AR_T is the friction resistance only; R_T · V is the brake power at the engine
  2. BR_T is the force that opposes the motion of the hull through the water at speed V (friction, waves, form, appendages); R_T · V is the effective power P_E, the power needed to tow the ship✓ answer
  3. CR_T is the thrust of the propeller; R_T · V is the delivered power P_D
  4. DR_T is the wave resistance only; R_T · V is the installed power SMCR
Solution
  1. R_T is the whole force of the water against the hull at speed V: friction, waves, form, appendages.
  2. Force × speed = power: P_E = R_T · V, the effective (towing) power.
  3. Example: 268.3 kN · 7.20 m/s = 1,932 kW.
Why not the others
  • A. R_T is more than friction, and P_E is before the losses.
  • C. Thrust is a different force.
  • D. Waves are only part of R_T; SMCR includes margins.
Remember: P_E = R_T · V.

Paper ship, 25 kn (1 kn = 0.5144 m/s): R_T = 1,792 kN. What is the effective power P_E = R_T · V?

  2. BP_E ≈ 44,800 kW

  4. DP_E ≈ 11,520 kW
Solution
  1. V = 25 · 0.5144 = 12.86 m/s.
  2. P_E = 1,792 kN · 12.86 m/s = 23,045 kW (kN · m/s = kW).

Quiz 2 · 16 · What the power does not include · Holtrop–Mennencalculator

For the paper ship the chain gives a shaft power P_S ≈ 32,600 kW at 25 kn, in calm water and with a clean hull. A sea margin of 15 % is added, and the engine is to be used at only 90 % of its MCR. What installed power (SMCR = P_S · 1.15 / 0.90) does this ask for?

  1. A≈ 37,500 kW
  2. B≈ 33,700 kW
  3. C≈ 29,300 kW
  4. D≈ 41,700 kW✓ answer
Solution
  1. Sea margin: × 1.15. Engine at 90 % MCR: ÷ 0.90.
  2. SMCR = 32,600 · 1.15 / 0.90 = 32,600 · 1.278 = 41,656 ≈ 41,700 kW.
Why not the others
  • A. Forgets ÷ 0.90.
  • B. Multiplies by 0.90 instead of dividing.
  • C. Multiplies by 0.90 only.
Remember: Margins raise the power: × 1.15, ÷ 0.90.
Quiz 2 · 17 · The Admiralty constant · Reading 2.3calculator

The parent is the reference ship: Δ = 13,200.7 t, P = 3,500 kW at V = 14 kn. What is its Admiralty constant C = Δ^(2/3) · V³ / P (Δ in t, V in kn, P in kW)?

  1. AC ≈ 59.6
  2. BC ≈ 31.3
  3. CC ≈ 18.5
  4. DC ≈ 438✓ answer
Solution
  1. Δ^(2/3) = 13,200.7^(2/3) = 558.6.
  2. V³ = 14³ = 2,744.
  3. C = 558.6 · 2,744 / 3,500 = 438 (typical range 400–600).
Why not the others
  • A. Uses V in m/s.
  • B. Uses V² instead of V³.
  • C. Uses Δ^(1/3) instead of Δ^(2/3).
Remember: C = Δ^(2/3) V³ / P, with V in knots.
Quiz 2 · 18 · Sister ship · Reading 2.3calculator

A sister ship of the same hull form floats 5 % heavier than the reference ship (Δ = 13,860.7 t). What power does she need at 14.5 kn, if the parent needs 3,500 kW at 14 kn?

  1. AAbout 4,083 kW
  2. BAbout 3,889 kW
  3. CAbout 4,501 kW
  4. DAbout 4,017 kW✓ answer
Solution
  1. Same hull form: C is the same, so P₂ / P₁ = (Δ₂/Δ₁)^(2/3) · (V₂/V₁)³.
  2. (1.05)^(2/3) = 1.033; (14.5/14)³ = 1.111.
  3. P₂ = 3,500 · 1.033 · 1.111 = 4,017 kW.
Why not the others
  • A. Uses Δ to the power 1.
  • B. Forgets the displacement.
  • C. Uses Δ³.
Remember: Displacement enters with 2/3, speed with the cube.
