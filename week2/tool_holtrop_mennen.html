Holtrop–Mennen Power

Calm-water resistance and propulsion factors by Holtrop and Mennen (1982), then installed power with your margins. It opens on the reference ship of Lectures 2.3a and 2.4 (the 10,000 t general cargo ship), used in Exercise 2.4. For your group's project, replace the inputs with your project ship's hull.

Hull
Bulb, transom, appendages

1+k₂: rudder behind skeg 1.5–2.0; twin-screw balanced rudders 2.8; shaft brackets 3.0; shafts 2.0–4.0.

Propulsion and margins
Speeds and parent ship

Admiralty column: Δ^(2/3)·V³/C with Δ in t, V in kn, P in kW. Take C from a parent ship of similar form and speed (in the project, from your D2 database).

Friction with form RF(1+k₁)Wave-making RWCorrelation, appendages, bulb, transom

Chain: PE = RT·V; ηD = η₀·ηH·ηR with ηH = (1−t)/(1−w); PB = PE/(ηD·ηS); SMCR = PB·(1 + sea margin)/engine load. η₀ is a fixed assumption in the baseline design (0.60, or a justified 0.60–0.65); the optimum Wageningen B-series propeller is selected in specialization SP02 (see homework H2b).

Use the method inside its data range: Holtrop (1984) gives the range per ship type. Outside it, compare with a second method (Lecture 2.4).

Source: Holtrop, J. and Mennen, G.G.J. (1982) An approximate power prediction method, International Shipbuilding Progress 29(335); Papanikolaou (2026) §2.14, footnote 34. The formulas reproduce the paper’s worked example (RT 1,792 kN at 25 kn).
